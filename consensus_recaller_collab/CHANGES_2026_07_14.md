# What changed in the consensus recaller, and why — 2026-07-14

A calibration audit found five defects. All five made the caller **over-call**,
and — this is the important part — **the existing control panel was structurally
incapable of detecting any of them.** All are fixed. Every previously published
panel is invalid and has been regenerated.

This document explains each change and the reasoning behind it. The measured
before/after evidence is in [`CALIBRATION_AUDIT.md`](./CALIBRATION_AUDIT.md).

---

## Why none of this was caught earlier

Every control in the old panel — the ±25/±50 bp boundary decoys, the
opportunity-matched local shifts, the pseudo-site nulls — is a **TF-geometry
control**. Each one compares a TF hypothesis against a *displaced TF
hypothesis*.

That means any bias shared by both arms **cancels exactly**. A bias that favours
TF-over-N is invisible to all of them, because both arms are TF.

Concretely: a genuine continuous nucleosome, run through the full production
gate, **passes the ±25/±50 decoy gate with a 25-log-unit margin** (the gate
requires 3). The headline "4 strict calls across 15,912 decoy scores" is a real
specificity result about *placement* — it says nothing about *splitting*.

There was **no true-nucleosome negative control anywhere**, which is exactly the
quantity the conservative end of the prior sweep exists to control. That gap is
why five over-calling defects coexisted with a clean-looking validation suite.
The first thing added was such a control (§6).

## 1. `sr`: every molecule was scored at one global deamination rate

**The problem.** A protected call is evidenced by the *absence* of deamination.
Its LLR is therefore a direct function of `P(hit | accessible)` — and the caller
used one global value for every molecule, with no per-read rate control of any
kind.

Estimating each molecule's own rate from its own accessible (`msp`) calls at the
DddB `ind` locus (n = 2,950):

| | 5th | median | 95th |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| real per-molecule `P(hit \| accessible)` | 0.093 | 0.387 | 0.517 |

The model's single global value is **0.497 — the 91st percentile.** It credited
nearly every molecule as if it deaminated like the top decile.

**Why this mattered so much.** `100% of strong strand rescues are zero-hit
calls.` Their entire evidence is "no deamination here." At the assumed rate that
is worth `+0.666` nats per missed base, so eight zero-hit opportunities always
scored `+5.33` — essentially the whole observed median strong-rescue logBF of
`+5.59`. A poorly-deaminated molecule looks protected *everywhere*, and the
caller read that as a TF footprint.

**The fix.** Estimate `P(hit | accessible)` per molecule from its own accessible
calls, shrunk empirical-Bayes toward the model's expectation *for that
molecule's own context composition* — so the factor measures enzyme efficiency,
not sequence content. Applied before any state model, prior, or proposal is fit,
so both passes see the same LLRs. `--global-deamination` restores the old
behaviour.

**Cost.** DddB `ind`: strong rescues **452 → 89**. Only **30 of 206** unique
strong rescues survive (15%). The 176 demoted calls had a median *old* logBF of
`+5.59` — they were not marginal, they were overcredited. The old model's
weakest "strong" call had **0.18 nats** of evidence.

**Caveat.** 4,282 of 8,683 reads have fewer than 20 accessible opportunities and
fall back to the global rate. Those keep the old behaviour.

## 2. `cr`: N and TF had incomparable geometry priors

**The problem.** The nucleosome hypothesis was marginalized over a **uniform
prior across every arithmetically possible (length, start)** — 887 geometries in
a typical case. The TF hypothesis was marginalized over its **empirical source
hulls**, all sitting in one place.

So N paid an Occam penalty of ~3.8 nats purely for having its prior *written
down vaguely*, and TF paid nothing comparable. That is a model-selection
artifact, not evidence.

**The test that exposes it.** Score a block with **no informative opportunities**
— every molecular LLR identically zero. A correct model must return the prior:

| nominal N:TF prior | prior implies | old model | fixed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1:1 | 0.500 | 0.949 | 0.597 |
| 10:1 | 0.091 | **0.652** | 0.129 |
| 100:1 | 0.010 | 0.158 | 0.015 |

The entire `+2.93` nat Bayes factor was geometry bookkeeping. The frozen "10:1
N:TF conservative baseline" was really operating at **~1.9:1 in favour of
splitting**.

Worse, the displacement scaled with the number of eligible N geometries, which
is clipped by read span: 34 vs 887 geometries gives TF posterior 0.15 vs 0.65 on
*identical* evidence. Short-read chemistries were therefore systematically more
conservative than PacBio — a chemistry-correlated confound in exactly the
cross-assay comparisons being made. Relabelling the prior axis could not have
fixed this.

**The fix.** Give N an **empirical geometry prior** drawn from source molecules
carrying an explicit nuc call over the same sites — the exact analogue of the TF
configuration library, with the same `-log(support)` normalization, the same
Gaussian call-edge kernel, and the same leave-one-molecule-out holdout. Both
hypotheses now carry comparably sharp, data-fitted priors. The uniform
enumeration survives only as a fallback below `--min-nuc-geometries` (default
20); the path taken is reported per candidate.

`test_uninformative_block_returns_the_prior_exactly` locks the invariant.

## 3. The local occupancy floor swallowed the prior sweep

**The problem.** The site-local Wilson occupancy prior was combined with the
requested global prior as `max(global_prior, wilson_lower_bound)`. Once the
Wilson floor exceeded the global TF prior — which it does at essentially every
site that makes calls — **the requested prior stopped mattering.**

In a random 20 kb region, at the requested **100:1** conservative prior, all
2,249 candidates had their prior overridden by the floor, and the effective odds
maxed out at 17:1 (median 6:1). The 10:1 and 100:1 scenarios produced
*bit-identical* counts.

**The conservative end of the sweep did not exist** — at exactly the sites that
make calls. This is the defect that most directly attacked the stated goal of
exploring the full aggressive→conservative range.

**The fix** (Tommy's call, from three options). The requested odds are now a
**skepticism multiplier** on the site's own occupancy prior, not a competitor to
it:

```
local_nuc_odds     = (1 - wilson) / wilson      # the site's own N:TF odds
effective_nuc_odds = local_nuc_odds x requested_odds
```

The floor sets the baseline; the sweep always moves it. `1:1` means "trust the
local occupancy"; `100:1` means "demand 100x more evidence than local occupancy
alone implies."

This is why **Homie went from 18 strong calls to 0**. Its Wilson LB is 0.344, so
its old effective prior was 1.91:1 — the floor had swallowed the requested 10:1
whole. It is now 19.1:1. Homie's calls were resting entirely on an un-swept local
prior.

## 4. Bridged complexes were licensing splits they cannot support

**The problem.** A *bridged* TF complex — two touching TFs with a protected
separator — protects exactly the bases a nucleosome would. It therefore predicts
**identical observations** to N, and no molecule can ever refute it. (This is
exact, not approximate: `interval_evidence` is additive, so the bridged term is
algebraically equal to the nuc-at-call LLR.)

That mass was being summed into `complex_posterior`, which drove the strict tier.
The result, found in the *regenerated* Nhomie panel: three strong calls whose
separator evidence pointed **at a nucleosome** yet scored TF posterior
0.990–1.000. The worst had **17 opportunities and 1 hit** in its separator — that
block is a nucleosome, and the model called it a TF pair at posterior 0.99.

**The fix.** Split the TF mass in two:

- **resolvable** — layouts whose internal separators are *actually observed to be
  accessible* on this molecule. Refutable by the molecule's own bases.
- **bridged** — the rest. Predicts the same bases as N; unrefutable in principle.

Only **resolvable** mass may license a strong split. Aggregate mass still drives
the *review* tier, because "this is not a canonical nucleosome" remains a useful
finding even when the exact layout is unresolvable. Both are reported.

After this, **all 13 surviving strong calls have genuinely accessible separators**
(2–5 m6A hits each, separator LLR −3.6 to −13.4 against a nucleosome). Zero rest
on bridged mass.

**What is *not* wrong here.** The bridge state itself is physically honest and
was left in place. Its evidence floor only binds when separator protection
exceeds ~1.4 nats, and with realistic dropout physics (a missed opportunity is
worth only ~+0.43 nats; an observed hit ~−2.9) a short separator cannot reach
that. Split support falls monotonically as missed separator opportunities
accumulate, exactly as intended. An earlier draft of this audit wrongly called
this fatal; it is not. The defect was in *what the bridged mass was allowed to
license*, not in modelling it.

## 5. Validation q-values were not valid FDR quantities

Two independent problems:

- **The null was not exchangeable across loci.** `normalized_delta` is
  `logBF / informative`, which is not depth-invariant — the BIC penalty per
  molecule and the sampling error of a per-molecule likelihood both scale with
  depth, so per-locus null *widths* varied ~2x. Pooling them made the shared null
  too narrow for some loci and too wide for others, so the p-values were not
  super-uniform and BH inherited no guarantee.
- **The 25 bp dedup median-averaged distinct decoys.** The code assumed the same
  genomic decoy gets reused across neighbouring parents; in the production data
  there are **zero exact-duplicate control intervals**. It was smoothing together
  genuinely distinct nulls, shrinking null variance while the observed statistic
  got no such averaging.

**The fix.** Studentize each observation by its own stratum's robust null spread
(MAD, family-level fallback) before pooling; keep one representative null per bin
instead of median-averaging.

Per-locus type-I error at the pooled 5% threshold (827-candidate panel):

| family / locus | before | after |
|---|---:|---:|
| hia5_pacbio / gm_napa | 14.3% | 4.3% |
| hia5_pacbio / gm_uba1 | 10.1% | 3.8% |
| **max deviation from nominal 5%** | **9.3%** | **5.0%** |

**Materially better, not perfect.** A few loci remain near 8% because null
*shapes* differ across loci, not only their scales. These q-values should still
not be presented as exact FDR. The BH implementation itself was always correct.

## 6. New: a true-nucleosome negative control

The control the panel never had. Six 20 kb random dm6 regions, five pooled PacBio
BAMs, production parameters, auto site discovery, **7,881 candidate blocks**. dm6
is overwhelmingly nucleosomal, so a decoy-gated strong split in a randomly chosen
region is a false split at high probability.

| requested N:TF | tested | MAP complex | strong | false-split rate |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1:1 | 7,881 | 97 | 30 | **0.38%** |
| 10:1 | 7,881 | 47 | 13 | **0.16%** |
| 100:1 | 7,881 | 21 | 8 | **0.10%** |

The sweep is now monotone and the conservative end genuinely suppresses false
splits. Before these fixes the same panel gave 0.44% at 10:1 and an *identical*
0.40% at 100:1 — the prior had no purchase at all.

**Read this as an upper bound, not a point estimate.** Random dm6 regions contain
real regulatory footprints, so some of those calls are likely genuine. The number
to watch is the *ratio across the sweep* and against future model changes, not its
absolute value.

---

## 7. Also fixed

- **The annotator could destroy a source BAM.** `--output` was checked only
  against the single input being processed, so pointing it at a *different* BAM
  from the same report passed the guard and `os.replace`d it. Now every BAM the
  run reads (and its index) is protected, and the check runs before any
  filesystem mutation.
- **The consensus tests never ran by default.** `pyproject.toml` `testpaths` was
  `["tests"]`, so all 81 tests of a component shipping four console scripts were
  excluded from `pytest`. Now included.

## Regenerated results

Four boundary panels, production 10:1, same parameters as the archived runs.

| boundary | tested | MAP complex (old → new) | review (old → new) | **strong (old → new)** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Nhomie | 198 | 27 → 23 | 25 → 22 | 4 → **7** |
| Homie | 574 | 50 → 12 | 50 → 12 | 18 → **0** |
| SF1 | 1,590 | 65 → 25 | 65 → 22 | 11 → **1** |
| SF2 | 1,618 | 64 → 33 | 61 → 32 | 15 → **5** |
| **Total** | | **206 → 93** | **201 → 88** | **48 → 13** |

Two results deserve explanation rather than hand-waving:

**Homie collapsed to zero.** Its Wilson LB is 0.344, so the old `max(global,
wilson)` gave it an effective prior of 1.91:1 while 10:1 was nominally requested
(§3). Its 18 strong calls were resting on a prior that the sweep could not touch.
At a genuine 19.1:1 they do not survive. Homie's paired-site core remains the
strongest *review*-tier class; it is no longer a strong callset.

**Nhomie went up, 4 → 7.** This is the geometry fix (§2) working as intended, not
a regression. Nhomie has only 26–66 source-observed nuc geometries (vs Homie's
196–275), and they do not sit at these candidates' edges. The empirical prior
therefore says: *a nucleosome here would sit where nucleosomes here actually sit,
and this block's edges don't match* — which is real evidence against N that the
old uniform prior could not express. All 7 have accessible separators with 2–3
m6A hits.

Reports and TSVs: `consensus_validation_outputs/regenerated_20260714/`.

## Still open

- **Residual q-value miscalibration** (§5): a few loci near 8% at nominal 5%.
- **Sparse-accessible molecules** still fall back to the global deamination rate
  (§1) — about half the reads at `ind`.
- The **220 bp ceiling and 90 bp floor** remain hard bounds, contrary to the
  project's usual scoring-plus-`nq` preference. Blocks outside them are counted
  and skipped, never silently interpreted.
- The **negative panel is 6 regions on one chemistry**. It should be extended to
  DddA/DddB/Nanopore before any operating point is re-frozen.
- The **cross-assay hierarchy panel** and the **DddB/DddA/Nanopore strand-rescue
  panels** still need regenerating; only the four PacBio boundary loci and the
  `ind` strand-rescue comparison have been redone.
